Quantifying chicory in coffee with SWIR hyperspectral imaging

A summary of everything done in the Claude Code sessions for the NITC, NTNU and Coffee Board project, guided by Dr. Shihabudheen K V: what was built, the numbers, why each choice was made, what is still open, and where every file lives.

Camera HySpex SWIR-384, 288 bands, 1000–2450 nm usedCoffee bases robusta, arabica, arabica + robustaLast updated 11 Nov 2026

01Where it stands

Chicory can be measured in all three coffee bases to under 1 % error on scanning days the models never saw. A classifier names the coffee base first, a browser app runs the whole chain on a raw scan, and the end-semester deck is drafted.

02What was done, in order

  1. Midsem

    Robusta + chicory

    11 levels (0–50 %), 33 dishes, 3 sessions. Eight regressors and eight preprocessings compared under nested leave-one-session-out. PLSR on absorbance + offset won at 0.79 %.

    Open at midsem: an arabica blend passed every check but read 8–10 points low on the robusta model.

  2. 5 Nov

    Arabica scans processed

    Arabica arrived as raw camera counts, not radiance. The dark background stored in each file's header is subtracted, then the Spectralon strip gives reflectance (checked against the radiance route: correlation 0.999). 4,950 patch spectra, 33 dishes.

    Plain PLSR failed on arabica (2.61 %, a 4.4-point bias on the session-1 pure arabica); EMSC + PLSR reached 0.78 %.

  3. 6 Nov

    Literature methods and a preprocessing grid

    SO-PLS, EPO-PLSR, VIP selection, di-PLS, GPR, mixing models and a joint robusta + arabica PLSR, on both bases; then 9 preprocessings × 5 models on arabica. Nothing beat the per-base winners.

  4. 6–7 Nov

    The AR series

    First modelled as arabica vs robusta with the labels as given: every model missed by 20–30 %. The spectra showed the varying component behaves like chicory (oil bands track the label), so the series was re-modelled as an arabica + robusta base plus chicory. The 13:30 AR 50:50 set was excluded. Winner: first derivative on 1650–1800 and 2150–2400 nm + PLSR, 0.94 %.

  5. 7 Nov

    Classifier and web app

    A 3-class PCA-LDA classifier picks the base before regression. All models exported as linear weights into a browser app; its JavaScript engine matches Python within 0.51 % on 33 training dishes.

  6. 8–9 Nov

    Website redesign

    Home with a scanned wireframe bean, three separate learn articles, the scan workbench, coffee palette in light and dark.

  7. 9 Nov

    Final model check

    Every preprocessing × linear model, all three bases, same nested test. The three chosen models reproduced their published per-session errors exactly before the grid ran; none of 69 alternatives per base was significantly better.

  8. 9 Nov

    End-semester deck

    Built from the midsem deck. Draft 2 adds a dedicated winner slide with the maths for each mixture.

03Method

From scan to chicory %

  1. Counts → reflectance. Raw files: subtract the dark background from the header. Divide each pixel by the Spectralon strip in the same column, times the certified reflectance.

  2. Dish masking. Dishes found by a ring matched filter; the rim is eroded away and shadow and glare pixels are removed.

  3. Patches. 12 × 12 px patches averaged to one spectrum each, 266 bands from 1000 to 2450 nm.

  4. Preprocessing, chosen per base: absorbance + offset (robusta), plus EMSC (arabica), or first derivative on the chemistry bands (three-way blend).

  5. Trimming and super-patches. Worst 10 % of patches per dish dropped on spectral residual; 8 neighbouring patches pooled (about 25 mm).

  6. Model. PLSR on the super-patches; the dish result is the mean of its super-patches.

How every model is judged

Nested leave-one-session-out. Each base has three sessions; each round trains on two and predicts the third. Inside a round, settings are tuned on the training sessions only, holding out one concentration level at a time, and the simplest setting within one standard error of the best is taken. Trimming is rebuilt per round too. The reported number is the mean of the three held-out errors. A paired bootstrap over dishes decides whether one model is really better than another.

Why linear models win

Absorbance follows Beer–Lambert, so a blend's spectrum is a weighted sum of its ingredients and chicory % is a straight line in the spectrum. With 11 levels and 33 dishes per base against 266 bands, flexible models memorise dishes; trees can only return training averages for levels they never saw; RBF kernels lose their neighbours when a new day shifts the spectra. Random forest, boosting and RBF-SVR were 4–11 times worse in every base.

05Decisions and why

Switch models only on significance

With three sessions per base, models 0.1 % apart cannot be told apart. A new model replaces the current one only when the bootstrap says it is better and it wins in two of three sessions.

PLSR kept over linear SVR (robusta)

A statistical tie (−0.05 pp, 95 % CI −0.14 to +0.05). PLSR has one setting, was stable across rounds (6, 7, 6 LV) and its weights sit on the 1730 nm oil band.

EMSC for arabica

Arabica's scatter changes between sessions; plain PLSR read pure arabica 4.4 points low on a new day. EMSC removes baseline and scale and keeps the chicory direction.

AR series read as base + chicory

Treated as arabica vs robusta, nothing fitted (20–30 % errors) and the oil bands followed the label like chicory does. As a coffee base plus chicory, every check passes and the error drops below 1 %.

13:30 AR 50:50 excluded

Removed on the team's call. Its dishes are spectrally identical to the 13:30 "AR100" dishes, so the two labels cannot both be right.

7 Sep file-to-label mapping by dish count

The 6-dish 12:00 set is J, K, L, 50, 60, 70 and the 4-dish 13:30 set is 80, 90, 50, 100, following the number of dishes in each scan rather than the order the labels were first listed in.

Linear models only in the app

Every step is linear, so each model is exported as one weight vector and runs in the browser exactly as in Python.

06Open questions

Confirm

AR blend recipes. The chicory % of the AR series is taken as the label's second number. The lab notes on how these blends were made should confirm it; the arabica : robusta split of the base is not measured.

Confirm

Arabica dish order. The arabica dish labels were assumed to mirror robusta (4 Sep: 50 → 10 % from the strip down; 8 Sep: 45 → 5 %). The spectra support the direction, but nobody has checked it against the lab sheet.

Limit

Three sessions per base, levels tied to sessions. The mean error carries roughly ±0.1 to ±0.5 % across sessions, and day and level effects cannot be fully separated. Repeating two levels on another day would fix this cheaply.

Limit

0–50 % chicory only, lab blends only. Commercial packs have not been scanned yet.

Decide

Deck length. Draft 2 has 26 slides; dropping slide 10 (reflectance and masking) returns it to 25. The website is private until it is shared, and publishing it openly needs the guide's approval.

08Re-running the work

The system Python on the laptop has no pandas or scikit-learn. Use the Python in ~/Fabric_Project/.venv, which has both, for the notebooks and scripts.

PY=~/Fabric_Project/.venv/bin/python
cd "/run/media/riyanvarghese/RIYAN HDD/coffee data"

# final model check (about 15 min per base, run in parallel)
cd final_model_check
for F in RBCH ABCH ARC; do $PY -u run.py $F > ${F}_log.txt 2>&1 & done
$PY compare.py

# app models after any retraining
cd ../coffee_app && $PY train_app_models.py

Paths are written into the notebooks as /run/media/riyanvarghese/RIYAN HDD/coffee data. On another machine, change BASE in each notebook's setup cell.
